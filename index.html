We laid four city facility maps over the census and 24 wards to ask if care follows people.

The short version

What we counted

Every government health service on the city's own maps: 186 dispensaries (small clinics), 211 urban primary health centres, 31 public hospitals and 28 maternity homes. Some share a building, so together they fill 402 places.

What we found

Across Mumbai there is about one public health site for every 30,951 people. In R South it is one for every 46,082 people; in A ward, one for every 16,819. South Mumbai has more sites per person than the suburbs.

What it does not mean

Fewer sites per person is a reason to look closer, not proof that people go without care. A count of buildings ignores beds, doctors, opening hours, private clinics and travel time, and the population figures are from 2011.

We also built three computer models that read a patient's test results and estimate the chance of heart disease. They are a learning exercise, not a doctor. See how well they did

The city on a ward monitor

Each heartbeat is one of Mumbai's 24 wards, A to T. The taller the spike, the more public health sites the ward has for every lakh (1,00,000) people. Hover over a beat, or tab through them, to read each ward.

Ward pipeline, four stages

In plain words: one command turns the raw city maps and census tables into every number, chart and model on this site. The bar shows how long each step takes.

Under a second

First, read four KML layers, merge 54 shared buildings and add 97 census wards into 24.

Three seconds

Then compute rates, shares and gaps for every ward and draw twelve figures.

Thirty-two seconds

Next, fit K-Means for K from 2 to 6 and re-test each fit 230 times with new seeds, subsamples and noise.

Instant

Last, write the Power BI tables that feed the dashboard.

Segment widths follow the measured run time of the ward stages (0.6 s, 3.0 s, 32.2 s, under 0.1 s), with a floor so the short stages stay readable. The same command then trains and tunes the three heart-risk models, writes the Power BI tables and exports the models for the browser. 73 automated tests check the counts, the formulas, the classifiers, and that the browser copy of the models gives the same answers as Python.

Every ward, one click

The heart risk predictor reads like a patient chart: five sections, from the patient to the ECG and imaging. As you type, a bedside monitor shows the vitals and runs its ECG at the heart rate you entered. One click runs all three models.

The overview keeps the headline numbers and a ward lookup that opens any ward's facilities and hospitals list. The charts and the facility map live in a five-page Power BI report, embedded in the dashboard.

Three models, one patient

Next to the ward study sits a second, separate part. Three computer models look at twelve routine heart-test results, such as age, type of chest pain, blood pressure, cholesterol and an exercise ECG, and each estimates the chance of heart disease.

They learned from 800 patients and were then tested on 200 they had never seen. The patients are from one hospital in India and their records have no addresses, so this part is never linked to Mumbai's wards.

Words on this site, in plain English

Ward
One of the 24 areas the city corporation (BMC) uses to run Mumbai, named by letters such as A, F North or R South.
Dispensary
A small municipal clinic for everyday illness: check-ups, basic medicines and dressings.
UPHC
Urban Primary Health Centre: a government neighbourhood health centre with a doctor, vaccinations, and care for mothers and children.
Public hospital
A hospital run by the state government or the BMC, from local general hospitals to big teaching hospitals such as KEM and J.J.
Maternity home
A municipal hospital for pregnancy and childbirth.
Public health site
One building with at least one of the services above. A dispensary and a UPHC in the same building count as one site.
Per lakh people
How many sites there are for every 1,00,000 residents. It lets big and small wards be compared fairly.
Share gap
A ward's share of Mumbai's people minus its share of the health sites. R South has 5.6% of the people but 3.7% of the sites.
Census 2011
India's official population count. It is the most recent one with numbers for each ward, so the populations here are from 2011.
Model
A computer program that learned patterns from past patient records and uses them to estimate the chance of heart disease for a new one.
Training and testing
The models learned from 800 patients and were then checked on 200 they had never seen, like an exam with new questions.
Accuracy
Out of the 200 test patients, the share the model got right. 95.5% means 191 of 200.
Recall
Of the test patients who really had heart disease, the share the model caught.
ROC-AUC
How well a model ranks sick patients above healthy ones: 0.5 is a coin toss, 1.0 is perfect.
F1
One score that balances catching sick patients against raising false alarms. 1.0 is perfect.
K-Means clustering
A method that sorts wards into groups with a similar mix of health facilities. It describes, it does not judge.
